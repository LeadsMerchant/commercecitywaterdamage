Water Damage Restoration in Commerce City

The first day after a leak decides how much of your floor, drywall, and cabinets can be saved. Our free line connects Commerce City homeowners with independent restoration providers who extract water and dry the structure.

Most water damage in Commerce City does not come from a river. It comes from a washing machine hose that let go, a water heater that finally rusted through in the basement, an ice maker line behind the fridge, or a toilet supply line under a bathroom vanity. Water from those sources is usually clean when it starts, but it travels under baseboards and into the subfloor faster than most people expect.

What a restoration provider usually does

Independent restoration companies generally follow the same order of work, adjusted to your home:

  1. Stop and assess. Confirm the source is off and map where moisture went using meters and, often, thermal cameras.
  2. Extract. Pull standing water out of carpet, pad, and hard floors.
  3. Remove what can’t be saved. Wet carpet pad, swollen particleboard, and the bottom of soaked drywall are common cuts.
  4. Dry. Set air movers and dehumidifiers and take readings until materials reach normal moisture levels.
  5. Document. Photos and moisture logs that insurers commonly ask for.

Why “looks dry” is not the same as dry

Colorado air is dry, so carpet and painted walls can feel normal within a day or two. The water underneath, in pad, tack strip, bottom plates, and wall cavities, takes much longer. That hidden moisture is what leads to odor, warped floors, and mold weeks later. It is fair to ask any provider how they will measure dryness and when they will remove the equipment.

Clean water, gray water, and black water

Providers sort water by contamination. A supply line break is usually clean. Dishwasher or washing machine discharge is “gray.” Anything that came up through a floor drain or toilet is treated as contaminated and needs a different process; see our sewage backup cleanup page. Tell the person you talk to where the water came from so they can plan correctly.

Homes across the city

Whether you are in an older home in the south end near Derby and Rose Hill or a newer build in Reunion, Turnberry, or Belle Creek, the first steps are the same: stop the water, keep people away from wet electrical, take photos, and get the drying started.

What you can do right now

  • Shut off the fixture valve or the main shutoff, often in the basement or near the water heater.
  • Turn off breakers to wet areas if you can reach the panel without standing in water.
  • Lift furniture onto foil or blocks and move rugs, papers, and electronics out.
  • Photograph everything before anything is thrown away.

Quick answers

How fast should drying start?

As soon as possible. Many providers aim to start extraction and drying the same day because materials that stay wet longer are more likely to need replacement.

Do you fix the pipe too?

No. Restoration companies handle the water damage. A licensed plumber usually repairs the pipe or appliance connection. Ask who handles each part of the job.
